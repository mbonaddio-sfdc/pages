Ministry for Pacific Peoples — process architecture

A holistic first-principles view of the services offered and the internal processes required to deliver them. Structured as a service catalogue and process library from Level 1 (domains) to Level 3 (processes). Purpose: to surface which processes are genuinely common across small “people ministries” — and are therefore candidates for a shared blueprint under the certification framework.

Version 0.1 · working outside-in view · to be validated with MPP
Caveat. This is an outside-in view built from public information about MPP's statutory mandate, published strategy documents, funding programs, and typical ministry-of-advocacy patterns. It is not a validated internal view. Some named programs (Toloa Fund, Language Weeks, Pacific Business Trust support) are drawn from public information; other process elements are generalised from what a ministry of this shape typically does. Every element here should be checked with MPP before being used in a Cabinet paper or investment case.

01Mandate context

The starting point for any process architecture is the statutory mandate — what the Crown has instructed this ministry to do. Every process below traces back to one or more elements below.

Statutory mandate

Principal advisor to the Crown on policies, programmes, and interventions to improve outcomes for Pacific communities in New Zealand.

  • Reports to the Minister for Pacific Peoples
  • Established as a departmental agency; ~100 FTE
  • Small policy shop with some direct-delivery programmes

Core functions

  • Policy advice to government on Pacific issues
  • Champion Pacific perspective across whole-of-government
  • Fund and administer targeted programmes
  • Convene Pacific communities and represent their voice
  • Monitor Pacific outcomes across sectors
  • Represent NZ at Pacific regional forums

Strategic frameworks

  • Lalanga Fou (four strategic goals for Pacific Aotearoa)
  • Pacific Aotearoa strategy — long-term direction
  • Pacific Wellbeing Strategy — cross-sector approach
  • Sector-specific Pacific strategies (Health, Education, etc.) — co-developed with lead agencies

02Level 1 — Process domains

Eight top-level domains describe the full activity of the ministry. Five are externally-facing (deliver something to citizens, communities, or other agencies). Three are internal (deliver capability to the ministry itself). All small ministries of this shape share this L1 pattern; differences appear at Level 2 and below.

D1
Policy & Strategic Advice

Advice to government, cross-agency policy contribution, strategic framework development.

D2
Community Investment

Grants, funding programmes, contracts with delivery partners. Where public money reaches communities.

D3
Community Engagement & Cultural Leadership

Stakeholder register, consultation, cultural events, language weeks, community forums.

D4
Cross-Agency Advocacy & Coordination

Sector-specific Pacific coordination (Health, Education, MSD, MBIE, Justice). Whole-of-govt outcomes monitoring.

D5
International & Diaspora Relations

Pacific Islands Forum representation, bilateral relationships, diaspora engagement, migration policy input.

D6
Monitoring, Research & Evaluation

Pacific outcomes data, research commissioning, programme evaluation, reporting.

D7
Ministerial Servicing

Correspondence, briefings, OIA requests, parliamentary questions, Select Committee support.

D8
Corporate Services

HR, finance, IT, records, communications, governance, health & safety. Small back office consuming shared services.

Common process candidates — where a shared blueprint pays back fastest

The processes below appear in near-identical shape across MPP, Ministry for Women, Whaikaha, and by extension most small policy-focused ministries. They are the strongest candidates for centralised reference blueprints under the certification framework — build once, deploy many, save Cabinet the cost of every agency doing it independently.

Grants & funding lifecycle

End-to-end grant admin — design, intake, eligibility, merit assessment, award, disbursement, milestone tracking, outcome reporting. Every small ministry does variants of this.

Salesforce PSS Grants blueprint

Stakeholder register & engagement

Person and Business Hub with relationship models, engagement history, community forum coordination, consultation events. Agency-specific overlays (Whakapapa, etc.) sit on top.

Salesforce Person + Business Hub

Ministerial servicing

Correspondence, OIA requests, briefings, PQs, Select Committee support. Almost identical across every ministry. Highest-value early consolidation opportunity.

Salesforce case + M365

Consultation & submissions

Public consultation event runs, submission intake, feedback synthesis, publication of responses. Same shape whether it's Pacific policy or gender policy.

Salesforce + NZ SDP

Contract & delivery-partner management

Delivery partner selection, contract negotiation, performance monitoring, variation, closure. Common to every ministry funding external delivery.

Salesforce + TechOne/Oracle

Cross-agency outcomes reporting

Data collection from other agencies via integration fabric, harmonisation, dashboarding, reporting to Cabinet. Same shape whether the lens is Pacific, women, or disabled people.

Data Cloud + MuleSoft + Tableau

Corporate services baseline

HR (Workday), Finance (TechOne), Records (M365 Purview), IT (ServiceNow), Website (SDP). Should never be built agency-by-agency for a ministry under 200 FTE.

Shared services stack

Research & evaluation

Research commissioning, external panel management, evaluation lifecycle, publication. Shared research commissioning platform reduces duplication.

Salesforce + TechOne + SDP

Annual reporting cycle

SOI, SPE, Annual Report, Estimates. Highly templated, ministry-agnostic in structure. Shared authoring & publishing platform cuts weeks of duplicated effort.

04What this suggests for the certification framework

Three implications for how GDA might sequence the common-process work under the certification framework:

1. Nine common blueprints unlock most of the value

  • The common process candidates above cover roughly 70–80% of what MPP does day-to-day, and the same proportion for Ministry for Women and Whaikaha.
  • Building these nine as certified reference blueprints delivers the Cabinet savings without needing every process to be shared.
  • Agency-specific processes (Toloa Fund, Nominations Service, Provider Oversight) sit as agency overlays on the same blueprints, not as separate builds.

2. Start with ministerial servicing, not grants

  • Grants is the most visible common process, but ministerial servicing (correspondence, OIA, briefings, PQs) is the highest-frequency and highest-commonality across all ministries.
  • Starting with ministerial servicing gets faster adoption because every ministry does it, every day.
  • Grants is a strong second — high value, but longer lifecycle and more agency-specific variation.

3. The reference blueprint is the common process

  • GDA's common process model doesn't need to be a separate policy document — it can be codified as the certified reference blueprints.
  • This side-steps the "8-levels-deep documentation" trap Andy is currently in. The process is the configuration.
  • Common process governance becomes: EDA maintains the blueprints, agencies adopt them, deviations require justification.
