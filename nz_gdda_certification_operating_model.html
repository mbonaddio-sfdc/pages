Certification body operating model

How the certification framework runs day-to-day — decision rights, submission and assessment process, investment allocation, tier movement, appeals, transparency, and cadence. Written for the people who would have to build and operate this inside GDA.

Version 0.1 · working document · companion to the certification framework

01Purpose and scope

This operating model turns the certification framework into a running institution. It defines who decides what, how vendors and platforms move through the certification lifecycle, how investment is allocated, and what the public register looks like. It's designed to fit inside GDA's existing back-office mandate, using existing budget lines, without needing new legislation.

In scope

  • All digital service capability domains listed in the CoE reference architecture (Citizen Service Delivery, Integration, Staff Service Management, HR, Finance, Productivity, Content, Identity)
  • Platforms sold or offered to NZ Government agencies for those domains
  • Central government agencies; Crown entities by opt-in; local government by future extension

Out of scope

  • Security accreditation (handled by existing NCSC / GCSB regimes; certification is complementary, not a substitute)
  • Individual agency procurement decisions (certification informs them; it does not replace them)
  • Vendor commercial contracting (handled through GDA marketplace as today, plus certified pricing transparency)

03Submission and assessment process

Rolling intake, not an annual window. Target 90 days from submission to tier decision. Two-stage assessment: gate criteria first (pass/fail), then stratifying criteria (Reference vs Approved).

Certification process — end to end
01 Submit Vendor lodges intent, conformance evidence pack, roadmap, pricing schedule. Rolling · vendor-initiated
02 Gate assessment EDA tests interop + sovereignty. Pass or reject. Target 30 days
03 Stratifying assessment EDA scores accessibility, blueprint, skills, transparency. Target 45 days
04 Tier decision EDA recommends Reference / Approved / Reject. Investment Committee ratifies. Target 15 days
05 Publish & onboard Decision published to register. Blueprint library stood up per tier. Agencies notified. Rolling

Vendor evidence pack — what's required at submission

  • Interop conformance: API specs, event schema, identity federation model, canonical schema mapping
  • Sovereignty: data residency statement, sub-processor list, foreign-access disclosure
  • Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA conformance report, NZSL/Te Reo/Easy Read pathways
  • Blueprint contribution: catalogue of reusable patterns the vendor commits to depositing
  • Skills: local partner ecosystem, certified practitioner numbers, training pathway
  • Commercial: published pricing schedule, roadmap for next 24 months, contract terms baseline

Assessment method

  • Gate criteria: Independent technical conformance testing. Third-party accredited tester where possible; EDA panel where not.
  • Stratifying criteria: Scored by EDA against published rubric. Scoring rubric public; scores published post-decision.
  • Reference vendors: Meet all six criteria at depth. Score ≥85% on stratifying criteria.
  • Approved vendors: Pass gates. Score 60–84% on stratifying criteria.
  • Rejected: Fail gates OR score <60% on stratifying. Feedback provided; re-submission allowed after 6 months.

04Investment allocation

Central investment is the mechanism that differentiates certification from a marketplace listing. Investment scales with adoption and with tier. Reviewed annually by the Investment Committee. Funded from GDA's back-office mandate budget — replacing distributed agency spend on duplicate capability.

What central investment funds

  • Reference blueprint library — shared configurations, accelerators, integration patterns
  • Shared talent pool — practitioners agencies can consume
  • Shared release & DevSecOps pipeline — one release train, many agencies
  • Sandbox and reference environments — for pattern development and agency onboarding
  • Community of practice — cross-agency user groups, knowledge sharing
  • Vendor liaison & standards evolution — ongoing operating cost of the certification body itself

How investment is scaled

  • Reference tier: Full CoE support — comprehensive investment across all six areas.
  • Approved tier: Lighter investment proportional to adoption. Starts modest; grows if agency uptake grows.
  • Assessing: Investment limited to conformance testing costs.
  • Divergent: No central investment. Agencies pay full cost independently.
  • Trigger for uplift: When cumulative agency adoption on an Approved platform crosses a defined threshold, investment Committee considers promotion to Reference.

05Tier movement — promotion, demotion, sunset

Tier status is not permanent. Movement is triggered by adoption change, standards evolution, or vendor conformance drift. Public register reflects current tier at all times. Grandfathering and migration support ensure agencies aren't left stranded.

Promotion
Approved → Reference

Approved platform demonstrates depth on stratifying criteria and sustained agency adoption. Investment Committee approves uplift; blueprint library and shared talent scaled up.

Trigger: Adoption threshold + recertification score ≥85%.
Demotion
Reference → Approved

Reference platform slips on criteria — commercial transparency weakens, blueprint contribution stalls, skills pool shrinks. Investment scaled down; new adoption steered to alternatives.

Trigger: Recertification score drops below 85% on stratifying criteria.
Decertification
Approved → Non-certified

Platform fails gate criteria at recert (interop, sovereignty), or vendor withdraws. Existing agencies grandfathered on defined runway; no new certified adoption.

Trigger: Gate failure or vendor exit. 2–3 year grandfather runway.
Sunset
Managed exit

Investment Committee decides to sunset a platform for strategic reasons (obsolete capability, better alternatives). Public notice + migration funding for existing agencies.

Trigger: Investment Committee decision. Cabinet-informed.

06Appeals and transparency

Legitimacy depends on public visibility of every decision and a real avenue for challenge. Two mechanisms: an independent appeals panel that can overturn certification outcomes, and a public register that discloses tier, criteria scores, and adoption.

Appeals

  • Vendors may appeal certification decisions within 30 days of publication
  • Appeals heard by independent panel — not the EDA, not the Investment Committee — reporting to GDA Leadership
  • Panel includes external technical + procurement expertise
  • Appeal outcome and rationale published
  • Appeals process funded by GDA; no vendor fee

Public register & disclosure

  • Real-time register of all certified platforms, tier, and effective dates
  • Published assessments for each certified platform (commercial-in-confidence redactions where necessary)
  • Adoption metrics per platform per domain — quarterly refresh
  • Aggregate central investment amounts per tier — annual disclosure
  • Vendor Advisory Panel meeting minutes published

08Establishment path

Standing up the certification body is a 12–18 month exercise. Phased so that first certifications can be granted in Year 1 while the framework matures.

Phase 1 — Foundation (Months 0–6)

  • Publish interop pattern library v1.0 through the EDA
  • Establish Investment Committee terms of reference
  • Stand up Vendor Advisory Panel with initial cohort
  • Publish criteria, scoring rubric, and evidence-pack template
  • Open rolling submission intake
  • First cohort of assessments in flight

Phase 2 — Certification (Months 6–12)

  • First tier decisions published to register
  • Blueprint library scaffolding stood up for Reference-tier platforms
  • Shared talent pool established (initially through vendor + partner secondments)
  • Agency onboarding pathway published
  • First reference deployments (e.g. POC ministries) launched

Phase 3 — Operate & extend (Months 12–18)

  • Full quarterly rhythm operating (Investment Committee, Advisory Panel)
  • First annual recertification cycle complete
  • Public annual report published
  • Extension consideration for Crown entities and local government
  • Framework version 1.1 published incorporating first-year learnings

Critical dependencies

  • GDA capability build — need enterprise architects, procurement specialists, secretariat
  • Independent testing capacity — either build internal or accredit third-party
  • Cabinet endorsement of the framework as the delivery mechanism for Driving Down the Cost of Digital
  • Budget redirection from distributed agency spend to central investment
  • Initial vendor engagement — willing first-cohort submitters demonstrate market appetite
