Working proposal · for GDA consideration

A certification framework for NZ Government digital services

Standards. Investment. Signalling. A proposed mechanism for delivering Driving Down the Cost of Digital by concentrating central investment on platforms that meet published criteria — and letting agencies choose from a transparent, competitive certified list.

Version 0.1 · working document · not yet endorsed by GDA

01What certification is

Not a compliance stamp. Not a vendor endorsement. Certification is an investment mechanism: GDA publishes technology-agnostic interop patterns, assesses platforms against them, and concentrates central investment on the platforms that meet the criteria — thereby lowering the marginal cost for every agency that chooses certified. Three components have to work together.

S
Standards

Technology-agnostic interop patterns published by the Enterprise Design Authority — identity, canonical data, event contracts, security posture, sovereignty. The same patterns apply regardless of which certified platform an agency picks.

I
Investment

Central spend on shared infrastructure per certified platform — reference blueprint library, shared talent pool, release pipeline, sandbox environments. This is the piece today's marketplace and standards regimes are missing.

T
Tiered signalling

A market-legible label — Reference, Approved, Assessing, Divergent — that tells an agency what depth of centralised support it will get if it adopts a given platform. Honest, published, defensible.

02The four tiers

One or two platforms per capability domain hold Reference status; several may hold Approved; new entrants pass through Assessing; agencies remain free to choose Divergent, but with the cost profile that comes with not sharing the infrastructure.

Reference
Reference-certified

Deepest centralised investment. Comprehensive blueprint library. Shared CoE, talent pool, release pipeline. Fastest agency onboarding, lowest lifecycle cost.

What it earns: full CoE support and market recognition as the government-endorsed reference for its domain.
Approved
Approved-certified

Conformant to the same standards as Reference. Blueprint library partial and growing; investment scales with agency adoption. A genuine second option, not a runner-up.

What it earns: a defined path to Reference through adoption; participation in the shared services ecosystem.
Assessing
Under assessment

Vendor is in flight through the certification lifecycle. Not yet certified. Agencies may pilot but should expect divergent-cost profile until certification is granted.

What it earns: a legitimate, transparent on-ramp — no vendor is locked out.
Divergent
Non-certified

Agency choice permitted as long as the platform conforms to interop patterns and can participate in DPI. Agency owns full lifecycle cost; no shared infrastructure.

What it earns: unrestricted agency autonomy, with cost of independence transparent up front.

03What certification measures

Six axes. Two are gate criteria — pass/fail, non-negotiable. Four are stratifying — they determine whether a platform holds Reference or Approved status, and can move a platform up over time as its depth grows.

Gate 1

Interop conformance

Federated identity, canonical data schema, event contracts, API standards, security posture. Can the platform participate in DPI at the level the Enterprise Design Authority defines?

Gate 2

Sovereignty & residency

Where does the data live? Who has access? What's the sub-processor chain? Does the platform accommodate NZ Government data sovereignty and foreign-access constraints?

Strat 1

Accessibility

WCAG 2.2 AA as a floor. Native pathways for NZSL, Te Reo, Easy Read, and assistive technology. Assessed at the platform level, not just at the customer implementation level.

Strat 2

Reference blueprint contribution

Can the vendor deposit reusable patterns, configurations, and accelerators into the shared library? Certified platforms have to be build-once, deploy-many. Depth here stratifies Reference from Approved.

Strat 3

Skills availability in NZ

Can agencies hire people to run this? Local partner ecosystem, certified practitioners, training pipeline. Global platforms with no NZ ecosystem struggle here.

Strat 4

Roadmap & commercial transparency

Platform roadmap visible to GDA. Pricing model public. No differential pricing between agencies. This is the criterion that most obviously supports Cabinet's cost-transparency goals.

04Governance — who runs certification

Three bodies inside GDA, deliberately separated so that technical assessment, commercial investment, and vendor consultation each have their own decision rights. Independence from vendor capture is designed in, not asserted.

Technical body
Enterprise Design Authority

Publishes interop patterns. Runs conformance assessments. Grants and revokes certification. Vendor-neutral, independent from any single agency.

Enterprise architects · security architects · data architects
Commercial body
Investment Committee

Decides how much central investment goes to each certified platform based on adoption, criticality, and roadmap. Reports to Cabinet through GDA leadership.

GDA finance · Strategy Office · portfolio leadership
Consultation body
Vendor Advisory Panel

Certified vendors input on standards evolution and roadmap alignment. Consultative only — no decision rights. Exists to keep standards technically realistic without allowing vendor capture.

Certified vendors · rotating chair · GDA secretariat

05Certification lifecycle

Six stages from vendor submission to sunset. Explicitly not a one-time gate — annual recertification, publicly maintained tier register, and a defined deprecation pathway when a platform stops meeting criteria.

Certification lifecycle
01SubmitVendor lodges intent, conformance evidence, roadmap.
02AssessEDA runs conformance testing against published patterns.
03CertifyReference, Approved, or Rejected outcome. Rationale published.
04InvestInvestment Committee funds shared infrastructure at the appropriate tier.
05OperateAgencies adopt through shared CoE, blueprints, and talent pool.
06Recert / SunsetAnnual recertification. Defined sunset pathway if criteria no longer met.
All tier decisions and rationale are published. Vendors can challenge outcomes through a documented appeal process.

06What each stakeholder gets

Certification only works if every party in the ecosystem has a rational reason to participate. The following are what agencies, vendors, GDA, and citizens each get from the framework.

Agencies

  • Faster onboarding via shared blueprints
  • Lower lifecycle cost — shared talent, shared release
  • Lower procurement risk — certified platforms pre-assessed
  • Retained autonomy to choose from a certified list

Vendors

  • Transparent market signal — clear pathway to certification
  • Reduced sales cycle — pre-assessed, agencies trust the tier
  • Participation in blueprint library — market visibility of capability
  • Fair competition — win on merit, not exclusion

GDA & DPI

  • Reusability at scale — build once, adopt many
  • Central IP retained in the service catalogue
  • Cost savings deliverable against Cabinet mandate
  • Levelled procurement — no silent picking of winners

Citizens

  • More consistent digital services across agencies
  • Stronger data protection through common standards
  • Faster delivery of new services as blueprints mature
  • Better accessibility as a floor, not a retrofit

07Precedents

Certification as a market mechanism has 20+ years of precedent across peer jurisdictions. This framework is a synthesis, not an invention.

  • UK Government — G-Cloud + Digital Marketplace + Service Standard. Tiered platform framework with published assessments and agency reuse. Directly analogous.
  • Australia — DTA Cloud Services Panel and the earlier Certified Cloud Services List. Federal-level tier system for cloud vendors with associated investment in shared capability.
  • India — India Stack certified integrators (NPCI). Private-sector implementers certified to build on national public infrastructure — direct analogue for how private-sector build participates in public digital services.
  • United States — FedRAMP. "Certify once, reuse many" pattern for cloud security across federal agencies.
  • NZ Health & Education sector accreditation regimes. Domestic precedent for standards + investment + public tiering, applied outside IT.
Why this delivers what Cabinet has asked for

The Cabinet paper Driving Down the Cost of Digital asks government to stop repeating investment across agencies for the same capability. Certification is the mechanism that operationalises that ask. Central investment concentrated on a small number of certified platforms replaces distributed spend on agency-by-agency rebuilds. Agencies choose from a transparent tier instead of running duplicate procurement processes. Vendors compete on published criteria instead of on relationships. The Cabinet savings target is achieved because the same digital capability is delivered many times over a shared foundation — rather than being reinvented every time.
