End-to-End Process Walkthroughs

Three agencies · one platform per process
Working sketch · v0.1 · for dialogue
One platform owns each end-to-end process. Salesforce is the anchor for intake, assessment, decision, fulfilment, notification, and closure. Other systems are called for specific capability the process consumes — payments, records, finance postings, cross-agency data — but the process itself is never handed off across a platform boundary.
The failure mode this avoids. A “customer experience” platform integrated to a separate “service delivery” platform with a different data model, workflow engine, and UX. The seam between them is always the failure point. See MPI.

What to notice. The complaint case and the provider contract case are linked inside Salesforce — one process feeds the other without a hand-off. Cross-agency referral (to MSD DSS, HDC, or Ombudsman) is a notification event, not a process transfer — Whaikaha retains oversight of its own case even when the substantive investigation moves elsewhere. The Enabling Good Lives outcomes loop runs entirely inside the same platform: every resolved case updates the outcomes evidence base without leaving Salesforce.
The pattern across all three. The Salesforce row is the only row present in every substantive process step. Every other lane is intermittent — called when needed, dark when not. That's what a process owned by one platform looks like on paper.

Where the MPI failure would have shown up. If we split the citizen-facing intake and the officer-facing service delivery across two platforms (as MPI did), the Salesforce row would go dark somewhere in the middle of the flow and a different platform would light up — with all the state, context, and data transfer problems that implies. Executives reading this should see the empty cells where those hand-offs would otherwise be.

Where the "one platform" principle explicitly relaxes. Finance postings, records retention, cross-agency data exchange, notifications, and payments are all handled by other systems — but they are transactional callouts, not process transfers. The process itself never leaves Salesforce. That's the line.
