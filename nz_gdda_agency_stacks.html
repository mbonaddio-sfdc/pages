Reference Agency Technology Stacks

Three ministries · greenfield
Working sketch · v0.1 · for dialogue

Three small “people ministries” on the certified reference stack in a greenfield build. Every layer below is a capability domain served by the platform holding Reference certification under the NZ Government CoE framework. Around 80% of each stack is shared services consumed identically; the differences sit inside the agency-specific overlays on the citizen-service platform and the community relationship model.

Guiding principle. One platform owns each end-to-end citizen-facing business process — intake, assessment, decision, fulfilment, notification, closure. It calls out to other systems for capability the process consumes (payments, records, finance postings, HR data). It never hands a live process across a platform boundary mid-flight.

How this connects to the CoE. Every platform named below is Reference-certified for its capability domain. An agency could substitute a certified alternate (e.g. MS Dynamics for Citizen Service Delivery, SAP SuccessFactors for HR) — they would still integrate into the same shared services and conform to the same interop patterns, but would carry the cost of divergence documented in the certification framework.

Reading the stack. Cells outlined with dashed borders on light-blue are shared services — consumed identically by all three agencies. Solid white cells are agency instances on Reference-certified platforms, with agency-specific configuration. Pink items inside cells are agency-specific overlays that extend a shared blueprint (e.g. Whakapapa relationships, accessibility-first UX, Enabling Good Lives principles).

The pattern that matters for GDA. Every citizen-facing service — grants, nominations, complaints, oversight — is delivered end-to-end on Salesforce (Reference-certified for Citizen Service Delivery). Salesforce owns intake, assessment, decision, notification, and closure. It integrates out for payments, records, finance postings, HR data, and cross-agency data exchange — but it does not hand the process off mid-flight to another platform. This is the difference between Service Victoria (works) and MPI's two-platform split (doesn't).

Staff service management is deliberately separate. ServiceNow (Reference-certified for Staff Service Management) handles staff IT, HR queries, and access requests — an internal-only capability. It is not on the citizen-service critical path. Executives who conflate ESM with citizen service delivery will read the stack and understand the distinction.

Choosing a certified alternate. An agency electing MS Dynamics 365 Customer Service instead of Salesforce, or SAP SuccessFactors instead of Workday, would produce a stack of the same shape at the same layers — but the blueprint library, shared talent, and integration acceleration would be thinner. The shared services (SDP, Identity, Notifications, Payments, MuleSoft, Data Cloud, M365) are unchanged. The cost of divergence sits inside the citizen-service layer, not the shared-service layer.
