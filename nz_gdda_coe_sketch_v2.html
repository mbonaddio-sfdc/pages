NZ Government Digital Services Centre of Excellence

Certified reference architecture
Working sketch · v0.2 · for dialogue

Standards first, vendors second. The Enterprise Design Authority publishes technology-agnostic interop patterns and certifies platforms against them. Certified platforms receive centralised investment in shared blueprints, talent, and release capability — lowering onboarding cost for any agency choosing certified.

What certification buys an agency. Faster onboarding (blueprint library exists), shared talent pool, shared release pipeline, and pre-integrated conformance to identity, data, event, and security patterns. Divergent choices are supported for interop — the agency just pays the full lifecycle cost of what centralisation would have provided.
Reference
Certified — Reference. Deepest centralised investment. Full blueprint library. Shared CoE, shared talent, shared release. Fastest onboarding, lowest TCO.
Approved
Certified — Approved. Conformant to EDA patterns. Blueprint coverage partial and growing. Onboarding real but slower than reference. Investment scales with adoption.
Assessing
Under assessment. Vendor in conformance testing. Not yet certified. Agencies may pilot but should expect the divergent-cost profile until certification is granted.
Divergent
Divergent. Not certified. Permitted if it conforms to EDA interop patterns. Agency owns full lifecycle cost. No shared blueprints, talent, or release capability.

Reading the sketch. Every column is a capability domain, not a vendor. Under each domain header the certified platforms are listed with their status: Reference is the platform with the deepest centralised investment, Approved is a certified alternate with a real (but thinner) blueprint library, Assessing is in-flight, and Divergent is anything else.

What this signals to GDA. The framework is technology-agnostic at the top: standards live in the Enterprise Design Authority and are the same regardless of which certified platform an agency picks. Certification is transparent — it names the platforms, publishes the cost-of-divergence, and lets agencies choose with eyes open. The Salesforce advantage is earned through the depth of the reference blueprint library, not asserted through positional exclusion.

How the model incentivises the right behaviour. The certification lifecycle rewards vendors who invest in conformance — patterns published centrally, adoption measured, investment scaled to adoption. Agencies who go divergent still get to plug into DPI (as long as they conform to interop patterns), but they carry the full lifecycle cost. That's the “lower cost through certification” story that connects directly to Driving Down the Cost of Digital.

The Salesforce play in this framing. Reference status in Citizen Service Delivery (with the deepest blueprint library any vendor currently offers), plus Reference in Integration Fabric via MuleSoft. Salesforce doesn't need to be everywhere; it needs to be the platform whose reference implementation is deepest, most reusable, and most upgradeable in its two domains. That's a defensible position that survives multi-vendor scrutiny.
